## Context & problem

QUINN explored a personal assistant that could recognize emotion in spoken commands. The project connected voice processing with a physical robotic interface, rather than treating the assistant as a screen-only system.

## My contribution

I integrated emotion-recognition work into an assistant and designed and produced its expressive robot head. The archived account describes a system that answers questions and responds to emotion in speech.

## Technical method

The work combined machine learning, speech, assistant behavior, and physical prototyping. The current CV describes embedding the model on Raspberry Pi. Designing the head required 3D modeling as well as a connection between software behavior and a visible physical response.

## Results & lessons

The surviving architecture and design images document the integration concept and hardware development. This project connects the separate voice emotion recognition experiments with a tangible human–machine interface. My chapter “QUINN – A Personal Assistant with AI” appears in *Digital Transformation in the Post-Information Age* (Softtrade, November 2022); the book’s contents identify the chapter and authorship.

## Limitations

The archive does not establish QUINN’s evaluation accuracy or exact project dates. Results from the separate emotion classification study cannot be transferred to the complete assistant. No clinical suitability or reliable emotional understanding is claimed.

## Context & problem

The research asked whether bioelectrical signals associated with stimuli could help identify a current mental state and potentially predict a subsequent one. This was an exploratory research question, not an established diagnostic capability.

## My contribution

I investigated this question as a student research project. The archive credits Zlatogor Minchev of the Bulgarian Academy of Sciences as consultant and Zvezdin Besarabov of University College London as mentor.

## Technical method

The available description links bioelectrical signal analysis to stimulus-associated mental states. The original illustrations provide context, but the extract does not establish a complete acquisition protocol, model specification, or evaluation procedure.

## Results & lessons

This early work introduced a research problem at the intersection of signals, learning, and human behavior. The documented contribution is the investigation and its hypothesis; no predictive accuracy is claimed.

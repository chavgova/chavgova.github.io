## Context & problem

Different circuit explanations can describe the same small neural network. This non-identifiability raises a practical question: which properties should an explanation satisfy, and how do those properties depend on what the explanation is for?

## My contribution

I reproduced an XOR experiment and investigated candidate explanations through sufficiency, necessity, faithfulness, structural soundness, generalizability, and minimality. I extended the original single-neuron logical abstractions to distributed multi-neuron representations.

## Technical method

The reproduction used small networks to examine explanation properties in a constrained setting. Controlled experiments evaluated how stronger interpretability constraints change the number and stability of valid circuits and explanations. A goal-relative framework distinguished understanding a model, steering its behavior, and reasoning about generalization.

## Results & lessons

The project developed an explicit way to discuss the purpose of an explanation before judging its quality. Reproducing a compact experiment also made the assumptions behind competing circuit accounts easier to inspect.

## Limitations

This is a research project, not an asserted peer-reviewed publication. Findings from a small XOR setting do not establish that the same conclusions hold for large language models. No quantitative result or external paper link is supplied.
